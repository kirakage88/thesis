# ChatGPT Suggestions

## Thesis Review
**Title:** *Development of a Predictive Maintenance Model for Coiled Evaporators in Split-Type Air Conditioning Systems*

---

# 1. Critique

## Research Gap

### Strength

The reframed research gap is significantly stronger than the original proposal.

Instead of arguing that scheduled preventive maintenance is insufficient, it identifies a more realistic operational problem:

> Scheduled preventive maintenance exists, but maintenance personnel lack continuous information about which specific units begin to deteriorate between maintenance intervals.

This shifts the study from attempting to replace preventive maintenance toward providing additional decision support, making the contribution much easier to defend.

---

### Potential Overclaim 1

Current wording:

> "...maintenance staff cannot identify which specific units are experiencing performance degradation between visits without physical disassembly."

This statement is too absolute.

Maintenance personnel may still detect degradation through:

- Occupant complaints
- Outlet air temperature measurements
- Electrical measurements
- Audible vibration
- Visual inspection

A safer wording would be:

> "...maintenance personnel have limited ability to consistently identify early-stage performance degradation without additional inspection."

or

> "...cannot reliably identify early-stage degradation between scheduled maintenance visits."

---

### Potential Overclaim 2

Current wording:

> "...prototype for scalable localized predictive diagnostics."

The study only develops one localized model.

It does **not** evaluate scalability.

A more accurate statement would be:

> "...serves as a proof-of-concept for localized predictive diagnostic models."

or

> "...demonstrates the feasibility of developing localized predictive diagnostic models."

---

# 2. Research Questions

## RQ1

Current:

> How effective are statistical feature selection and dimensionality reduction techniques...

Feature selection and PCA answer different research questions.

Feature selection identifies informative variables.

PCA transforms variables into lower-dimensional representations.

Consider separating the objectives:

- Which non-invasive sensor features contribute most to distinguishing Normal and Abnormal units?
- Can dimensionality reduction preserve classification performance while reducing feature dimensionality?

---

## RQ2

Current:

> Which classification model demonstrates the highest reliability...

The word **reliability** is ambiguous.

Specify the evaluation metric.

Examples:

- F1-score
- Balanced Accuracy
- MCC
- Recall
- ROC-AUC

---

## RQ3

Current:

> Does replacing raw environmental features with PMV improve classification?

This is the weakest research question.

PMV measures **occupant thermal comfort**, not equipment health.

A stronger justification is needed.

Instead:

> Can PMV, as a thermodynamically derived composite variable, replace multiple environmental sensor measurements without reducing classification performance?

This provides a clearer engineering rationale.

---

# 3. Hypotheses

## H1

Current:

> At least one ML model achieves F1 ≥ 0.85.

This is testable.

However, the threshold requires justification.

Explain why **0.85** was selected.

Possible sources:

- Previous literature
- Engineering performance requirement
- Similar predictive maintenance studies

---

## H2

Current:

> PCA-reduced features achieve comparable performance.

"Comparable" is subjective.

Instead define a non-inferiority margin.

Example:

> Performance differs by no more than 2% in F1-score.

---

## H3

Current:

> CNN outperforms tabular models by ≥2%.

The **2%** threshold appears arbitrary.

A better hypothesis would evaluate statistical significance instead.

Example:

> Integrating vibration spectrogram features significantly improves classification performance compared to tabular-only models.

---

## H4

Current:

> PMV does not significantly degrade classification performance.

This is a strong hypothesis.

It functions as a non-inferiority hypothesis and is appropriate if PMV is intended to simplify environmental sensing.

---

# 4. Methodological Loopholes

## 4.1 Labeling Strategy

This is the largest methodological concern.

The proposal does not explain how **Normal** and **Abnormal** labels are assigned.

Questions reviewers may ask:

- Who determines the labels?
- HVAC technician?
- Maintenance office?
- Manufacturer specifications?
- Performance testing?
- Cooling capacity measurements?
- Visual inspection?

Machine learning quality depends heavily on label quality.

---

## 4.2 Class Imbalance

Abnormal units may be rare.

Example:

- 35 units
- Only 5 abnormal

This creates class imbalance.

The proposal should discuss:

- Class weighting
- Balanced sampling
- Stratified cross-validation
- Oversampling techniques (when appropriate)

---

## 4.3 Weekly Sampling

Weekly visits may miss transient failures.

Example:

- Monday: Healthy
- Wednesday: Refrigerant leak
- Friday: Repaired
- Monday: Sample collected

The failure is never observed.

This limitation should be acknowledged.

---

## 4.4 Repeated Measurements

Repeated measurements from the same unit are **not independent observations**.

Example:

- 30 AC units
- 20 weeks
- 600 observations

This is **not** equivalent to 600 independent samples.

Random train-test splitting would allow the model to memorize individual units.

Instead, validation should use grouped methods such as:

- Group K-Fold
- Leave-One-Unit-Out Cross Validation

This is one of the most important statistical concerns.

---

## 4.5 Ground Truth Drift

Unit condition changes over time.

Example:

- Week 2: Normal
- Week 10: Abnormal
- Week 15: Repaired
- Week 16: Normal

The proposal should explain how changing labels will be handled.

---

## 4.6 Non-Invasive Constraint

The system intentionally excludes internal measurements.

Therefore it may not detect:

- Coil fouling
- Internal ice formation
- Fin blockage
- Internal refrigerant issues

This limits the achievable classification performance.

The proposal should explicitly recognize this limitation.

---

## 4.7 Localized Dataset

Training exclusively on Xavier University data is a strength.

However, it also limits generalizability.

The proposal should clarify that the model is intended for localized deployment rather than universal application.

---

## 4.8 PMV Experiment

PMV describes **human thermal comfort**, not evaporator health.

The engineering rationale should explain why PMV is expected to retain information useful for machine classification.

Otherwise reviewers may question its inclusion.

---

## 4.9 Portable Sensor Repeatability

Portable sensor kits introduce measurement variability.

Potential sources:

- Probe placement
- MPU6050 mounting position
- Wire routing
- Contact pressure

A standardized installation protocol should be developed.

---

## 4.10 Vibration Spectrogram

MPU6050 measurements may capture:

- Compressor vibration
- Fan vibration
- Building vibration
- Wall vibration

Sensor placement should therefore be standardized to avoid learning installation artifacts instead of fault characteristics.

---

# 5. Strengths Compared to the Original Proposal

## Non-invasive Design

- Easier deployment
- Easier ethics approval
- Easier acceptance by facilities management

---

## Portable Sensor Kits

- More feasible

The proposal no longer attempts to replace preventive maintenance.

Instead, it prioritizes maintenance inspections.

This is arguably the strongest conceptual improvement.

---

Using exclusively Xavier University data creates a realistic institutional model.

Although less generalizable, it provides practical value for campus deployment.

---

The revised proposal emphasizes:

- Electrical measurements
- Vibration analysis
- Engineering-derived variables (PMV)

The work now appears rooted in Mechanical Engineering rather than simply applying machine learning.

The revised proposal is significantly more focused.

It avoids attempting to simultaneously solve:

- IoT deployment
- Cloud systems

- Predictive maintenance
- Dashboards

# 6. Suggested Refinement of Research Questions

## RQ1

> Which non-invasive thermal, electrical, and vibration features contribute most to distinguishing Normal and Abnormal split-type air conditioning units, and can dimensionality reduction preserve classification performance while reducing feature dimensionality?

---

## RQ2

> Which machine learning classifier achieves the highest classification performance for identifying Normal and Abnormal unit conditions using exclusively non-invasive sensor measurements?

---

## RQ3 (Conditional)

> Can PMV, as a thermodynamically derived environmental feature, replace raw environmental sensor variables without significantly reducing classification performance?

---

# 7. Suggested Refinement of Hypotheses

## H1

> At least one classification model will achieve an F1-score of at least 0.85 in distinguishing Normal and Abnormal unit conditions.

---

## H2

> Models trained using PCA-reduced features will achieve classification performance within a predefined non-inferiority margin (e.g., ≤2% difference in F1-score) relative to models trained on the full feature set.

---

## H3

> Integrating vibration spectrogram features extracted from MPU6050 measurements with tabular sensor data will significantly improve classification performance compared with models trained using tabular features alone.

---

## H4

> Replacing raw environmental measurements with PMV will not produce a statistically significant reduction in classification performance, indicating that PMV can serve as a compact engineering-derived environmental descriptor.

---

# Overall Assessment

The revised proposal is substantially stronger than the original.

The shift from proposing a replacement for preventive maintenance to a complementary maintenance triage tool makes the research more realistic, feasible, and easier to justify.

The most significant remaining weaknesses are methodological rather than conceptual. These include:

- A clearly defined labeling protocol for Normal and Abnormal units.
- Appropriate handling of repeated measurements using grouped cross-validation.
- A strategy for managing class imbalance.
- A stronger engineering justification for the optional PMV experiment.

Addressing these concerns would significantly improve the scientific rigor and credibility of the proposed study while maintaining a scope appropriate for a bachelor's thesis.
